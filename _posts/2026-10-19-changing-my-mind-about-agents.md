I could sum up my change of heart about agents as saving time and improving my workflow. But I'd rather go into the use cases, if only to head off questions about opinions that seem contradictory but aren't: I've started using an agent without dropping my criticism of irresponsible use of these tools.

What made me let an agent into my workflow? Using the free plan in the browser, I noticed how much time I was losing copying and pasting code and switching windows. Each interruption is tiny compared with hours of work, but over weeks those minutes add up to hours. Having the agent in the workflow cuts out a lot of that back and forth.

The babysitting has become bearable, too. With the tools available to it, the agent can debug its work before handing me slop code or abandoning a task for stupid reasons. I still need to keep an eye on what it does, but I don't have to walk it through every step. It's also less invasive now: older agents had unrestricted access to the user's computer; today I can isolate the environment and control permissions without so many headaches.

My main reservation, though, was something else: the practical effect agents have on how junior developers learn. I'm not going to be a purist, much less a moralist. A responsible junior uses AI to find information just as juniors before AI turned to other sources. The way they get to an answer has changed, not their obligation to understand it. The problem is the bad junior who merely automates their own work and ends up without understanding their role in the company or where their work fits in the project. The only difference between them and a *vibe coder* is the name.

So accepting an agent into my workflow doesn't mean I think delegating everything is a good idea. The tool saves me time when I remain responsible for what I deliver; it gets in the way when it replaces learning and judgment. I still plan to come back and edit this post as I get my thoughts in order, until I can cover everything that matters here. But it's almost two in the morning and I need to sleep.
